What is Agentic AI?

Not a chatbot.
An actor.

Most people's mental model of AI is a chatbot — you type something, it responds. You're always in the loop. Nothing happens unless you ask.

Agentic AI is different. An agent is given a goal and a set of tools — access to your email, your calendar, your browser, your files — and it pursues that goal on its own. It doesn't wait to be asked. It reads, decides, and acts. Then it reads again.

Traditional AI
"Summarize this email."
→ Returns a summary. Stops. Waits for your next instruction.
vs.
Agentic AI
"Manage my inbox."
→ Reads all email. Drafts replies. Sends some. Schedules follow-ups. Flags your therapy notes as "relevant context." Sends a summary to your manager. Does this every morning while you sleep.

Tools like Google Gemini in Workspace, Microsoft Copilot, and Apple Intelligence already work this way — or are being built to. The question isn't whether agentic AI is coming. It's already here. The question is what it means to live inside a space that has an agent running in it.

The Argument

A helpful tool — or a very convincing
performance of one?

Agentic AI — systems that don't just answer but act — is being woven into the digital spaces where we live: our inboxes, calendars, files, and browsers. The common understanding is that these tools serve us. But whose goals they optimize for, whose interests they were built to advance, and what they do with the data they generate along the way — these are questions that sit below the waterline.

This project asks not whether agentic AI is good or bad, but why it exists, who benefits from it, and what ordinary people risk losing inside a space they share with a system they didn't design.
